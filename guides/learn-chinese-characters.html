How to Learn Chinese Characters

Characters are not 5,000 drawings. They are a few hundred components, recombined — which changes the whole learning plan.

The number that scares people is 50,000 — the size of a large dictionary. The number that matters is smaller: about 3,000 characters cover 99% of modern written Chinese, and the 300 most frequent ones already cover roughly half of the characters you will meet on a page. Characters are built from components, and once you can see the components the shapes stop being arbitrary.

Characters are components, not pictures

Most characters are made of two or more parts, each carrying either meaning or sound. 妈 mā (mother) is 女 'woman' + 马 mǎ, which is there for the sound. 钱 qián (money) is 钅 'metal' + 戋. Once you learn 女, 马, 钅, you stop memorising 妈 and 钱 as unique drawings.

  • 妈 = 女 + 马mā = nǚ + mǎ — mother: meaning 'woman' + sound 'ma'
  • 想 = 相 + 心xiǎng = xiāng + xīn — to think: 'appearance' + 'heart'
  • 河 = 氵 + 可hé = shuǐ + kě — river: water radical + sound

Stroke order: five rules

Stroke order is not decoration. It decides how handwriting looks, how handwriting recognition reads your input, and how you remember the shape. Learn it while the character count is small — relearning it at 800 characters is miserable.

  • Top to bottom: 三, 言.
  • Left to right: 你, 好.
  • Horizontal before vertical: 十, 干.
  • Outside before inside: 月, 问 — then close the box: 国, 回.
  • Middle before the two sides: 小, 水.

A method that survives month three

Learn words, not characters. 银行 yínháng (bank) is one word made of two characters you may already know; memorising 银 and 行 separately costs twice the effort and teaches half the usage.

Recognition before production. Reading and typing need recognition; handwriting needs production. Most learners need handwriting for a few hundred characters at most. Type pinyin and let the input method do the rest.

Review on a schedule, not on mood. Ten new characters a day with spaced repetition beats fifty on Sunday. Review is where the learning actually happens; the first pass is just exposure.

Read something you can almost read. Graded readers and the lessons on this site exist for this: text where you know 80–90% of the characters and the rest is guessable from context.

What not to do

  • Copying characters twenty times each. It feels productive and teaches your hand, not your memory.
  • Learning characters in frequency-list order with no words attached.
  • Waiting until you 'know pinyin properly' before starting characters. They reinforce each other.

常见问题 · FAQ

How many characters do I need to read a newspaper?

About 3,000 covers roughly 99% of modern written text. Around 1,000 is enough for simple news and graded readers.

Simplified or traditional characters?

Mainland China and Singapore use simplified; Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau use traditional. Learn the system used by the people you actually talk to — the shared core is large.

Do I need to handwrite?

Not for most goals. Reading and typing need recognition only. Handwriting helps memory and is required for exams and forms, so most learners handwrite a few hundred characters and recognise the rest.

Why do characters look like pictures but aren't?

A small number are pictographic (日 sun, 山 mountain). Most are compounds of a meaning component and a sound component, which is why learning components pays off so quickly.
