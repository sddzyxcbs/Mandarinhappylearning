A Practical Guide to Pinyin

Pinyin looks like English spelling and is not. Here are the sounds that actually differ, and the rules that make the rest predictable.

Pinyin is a romanisation system, not English spelling. Most syllables sound close to what an English speaker would guess, which is exactly the problem: the dozen that don't are the ones you will mispronounce for years if nobody tells you. This guide is about those dozen.

The shape of a syllable

Every syllable is an optional initial (consonant) plus a final (vowel or vowel + nasal). mā = initial m + final a. zhōng = initial zh + final ong. There are 21 initials and about 35 finals, and only around 400 syllables in total — which is why Chinese has so many homophones.

The single most useful correction: x/q/j are made with the tongue flat behind the lower teeth, not curled back. zh/ch/sh are the curled-back versions of the same places.

Finals worth extra attention

  • ü — say 'ee' then round your lips without moving your tongue. 女 nǚ, 绿 lǜ. After j, q, x, y it is written u but still pronounced ü: 去 qù, 鱼 yú.
  • -n vs -ng — 安 ān ends with the tongue on the ridge behind the teeth; 昂 áng ends in the back of the mouth. 分 fēn vs 风 fēng.
  • -ian — not 'ee-an' but closer to 'yen': 天 tiān, 钱 qián.
  • -ong — like 'oong': 中 zhōng, 红 hóng.
  • e — a back, unrounded 'uh': 喝 hē, 很 hěn.

Where the tone mark goes

Tone marks sit on the vowel in this priority order: a, o, e, i, u, ü. So hǎo (on a), duì (on i), liù (on u), lǜ (on ü). When i carries a mark, the dot disappears: nǐ. Neutral tone takes no mark at all.

Spelling rules that surprise people

  • No syllable starts with i or u alone: yi for i, wu for u, yu for ü.
  • j, q, x take u where the sound is ü: ju, qu, xu — never jü.
  • After y, ü loses its dots: yuan, yun.
  • Syllables are separated with an apostrophe when ambiguity would result: Xī'ān (Xi'an) vs xiān (fresh).
  • Names and places capitalise each syllable: Běijīng, Zhāng Wěi.

Typing Chinese

You do not need to learn a keyboard layout. Every major OS ships a pinyin input method: type nihao, pick 你好 from the candidate list. Type whole words rather than single characters — zhongwen gives 中文 immediately, while zhong asks you to choose among dozens. Tones are not typed; the input method guesses from context.

A 10-minute daily routine

  • Read one short passage aloud with tone marks — slow enough to hear yourself.
  • Record 30 seconds of it, then listen once. Do not loop on it.
  • Pick three syllables you fumbled and say minimal pairs for them.
  • Add any new word to your review list with its pinyin attached.

常见问题 · FAQ

Do Chinese people use pinyin?

Yes — as the input method for typing, for children learning characters, and in dictionaries. Taiwan uses zhuyin (bopomofo) for the same job, though pinyin is standard for learners.

Why is 去 written qu and not qü?

After j, q, x and y the ü sound is written u. It is still pronounced ü. The dots only appear after n and l: 女 nǚ, 绿 lǜ.

Do I type tones?

No. Pinyin input methods take unmarked letters and predict the characters from context. Type whole words — zhongwen gives 中文 faster than zhong.

Can I learn Chinese without pinyin?

You can learn to speak from audio, but pinyin is how you look words up, type them, and remember pronunciation. It is worth two weeks of focused attention early.
