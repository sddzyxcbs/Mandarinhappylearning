Chinese Sentence Structure

Chinese is subject-verb-object — until time, place and aspect particles start moving things around. Here is the frame.

The good news is that Chinese word order is closer to English than most languages: subject, then verb, then object. 我喝茶 — I drink tea. The work starts when you add when, where, how, and whether the action is finished, because Chinese handles all four with position and small particles rather than with verb endings.

The base: subject + verb + object

No conjugation, no articles, no plural endings. 我 hē chá works for I drink, you drink, we drank, they will drink — tense comes from context words like 昨天, 明天, or from aspect particles.

  • 我喝茶。wǒ hē chá. — I drink tea.
  • 他学中文。tā xué Zhōngwén. — He studies Chinese.
  • 我们看电影。wǒ men kàn diàn yǐng. — We watch a film.

Time goes before the verb, not at the end

English puts 'tomorrow' anywhere; Chinese wants it after the subject and before the verb. The same slot holds longer time phrases: 我每天早上七点起床 — I get up at seven every morning.

  • 我明天去北京。wǒ míng tiān qù Běijīng. — I'm going to Beijing tomorrow.
  • 他昨天没来。tā zuó tiān méi lái. — He didn't come yesterday.

Place uses 在 and sits before the verb too

  • 我在家吃饭。wǒ zài jiā chī fàn. — I eat at home.
  • 他住在上海。tā zhù zài Shànghǎi. — He lives in Shanghai.
  • 书在桌子上。shū zài zhuō zi shang. — The book is on the table.
Where English says 'eat at home', Chinese says 'at home eat'. Putting the place after the verb (我吃饭在家) is one of the most recognisable beginner errors.

Adjectives need 很 — and never 是

In Chinese an adjective is a verb-like predicate; a bare adjective sounds unfinished, so 很 fills the slot. It does not necessarily mean 'very'. And do not say 我是好 — 是 links nouns to nouns, not nouns to adjectives.

  • 我很好。wǒ hěn hǎo. — I'm fine.
  • 今天很冷。jīn tiān hěn lěng. — It's cold today.
  • 这个菜很好吃。zhè ge cài hěn hǎo chī. — This dish is delicious.

Possession with 的

  • 我的书wǒ de shū — my book
  • 老师的名字lǎo shī de míng zi — the teacher's name
  • 红色的车hóng sè de chē — a red car

Questions: three shapes

  • Yes/no with 吗: 你喜欢茶吗？ nǐ xǐ huan chá ma? — statement plus 吗, word order unchanged.
  • Question words stay in place: 你叫什么名字？ nǐ jiào shén me míng zi? — literally 'you called what name'. There is no do-support and no inversion.
  • Choice with 还是: 你喝茶还是咖啡？ nǐ hē chá hái shi kā fēi? — tea or coffee?

Negation splits in two

不 bù negates present, habitual and future, and adjectives: 我不喝酒 (I don't drink), 他不高 (he isn't tall). 没 méi negates 有 and completed actions: 我没有钱 (I don't have money), 我没去 (I didn't go). Saying 不有 or 没去 for a habitual action both mark a beginner.

Aspect: 了, 过, 正在

了 marks a completed action, 过 marks experience, 正在 marks something in progress. None of them is a past tense — Chinese does not have one. 明天我吃了饭就去 is perfectly normal Chinese about the future.

  • 我吃了。wǒ chī le. — I've eaten / I ate.
  • 我去过中国。wǒ qù guo Zhōngguó. — I've been to China.
  • 他正在打电话。tā zhèng zài dǎ diàn huà. — He's on the phone right now.

The order of everything

When a sentence gets busy, the skeleton is: subject + time + [在 + place] + [跟 + person] + verb + aspect + object. 我明天在家跟他吃饭 — I'm eating with him at home tomorrow. Fix the skeleton and the vocabulary can be replaced freely.

常见问题 · FAQ

Is Chinese really subject-verb-object?

The basic order is SVO, but topic-comment structures are extremely common: 这个菜，我很喜欢 (this dish, I like very much). Learn SVO first; topic-comment comes with exposure.

Why can't I say 我是高 for 'I am tall'?

是 links nouns to nouns. Adjectives are predicates on their own and take 很 as a placeholder: 我很高.

Where does 了 go?

After the verb for a completed action (我吃了饭), or at the end of the sentence when it marks a change of state (下雨了).

Do Chinese verbs conjugate?

No. There are no tense or person endings at all. Time is carried by words like 昨天, 明天, and by aspect particles such as 了 and 过.
