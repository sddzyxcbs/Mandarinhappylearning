Chinese Numbers from 1 to 100

The counting system is more regular than English. The traps are 两, 万, money, and reading a phone number aloud.

Mandarin numbers are refreshingly logical: learn ten words and you can say any number under a hundred by stacking them. Then a few irregularities appear — 两 instead of 二, ten thousand instead of a million, and 幺 on the phone — and those are exactly the ones that come up when you are paying for something.

1 to 10

四 sì (four) and 十 shí (ten) are the two that English speakers mix up. 四 is a sharp falling tone; 十 is a rising one.

11 to 99: stacking

Eleven is 'ten one' — 十一 shí yī. Twenty is 'two ten' — 二十 èr shí. Twenty-five is 'two ten five' — 二十五 èr shí wǔ. There are no irregulars in this range at all, which is why counting to 99 is a one-afternoon job.

  • 十五shí wǔ — 15
  • 三十八sān shí bā — 38
  • 九十九jiǔ shí jiǔ — 99

Hundreds, thousands, and the 万 trap

Chinese groups numbers by four digits, not three. 百 bǎi is hundred, 千 qiān is thousand, 万 wàn is ten thousand, and 亿 yì is a hundred million. There is no word for 'million' — 100,000 is 十万 shí wàn.

  • 一百yì bǎi — 100
  • 三百五sān bǎi wǔ — 350 (the final digit is dropped in speech)
  • 一千yì qiān — 1,000
  • 一万yí wàn — 10,000
  • 两万五liǎng wàn wǔ — 25,000
Spoken Chinese routinely omits the last unit: 三百五 means 350, not 305. In prices and quantities this is the normal form, and it is a common source of confusion for learners.

二 or 两?

Both mean two. 二 èr is used for counting, in dates, in phone numbers, and in numbers above 100 when it is not followed by a measure word. 两 liǎng is used before a measure word — which is most of the time when you are actually buying something.

  • 两个人liǎng ge rén — two people
  • 两杯咖啡liǎng bēi kā fēi — two cups of coffee
  • 二月二号èr yuè èr hào — February 2nd
  • 二十èr shí — twenty (never 两十)

Money

The currency unit is 元 yuán, but in speech everyone says 块 kuài. One tenth is 毛 máo (written 角), one hundredth is 分 fēn, which is now effectively gone.

  • 多少钱？duō shao qián? — How much is it?
  • 三十五块sān shí wǔ kuài — 35 yuan
  • 八块五bā kuài wǔ — 8.50 yuan
  • 太贵了tài guì le — Too expensive

Phone numbers

Phone numbers are read digit by digit, and 1 is usually 幺 yāo rather than 一 yī — because 一 and 七 can blur on a bad line. 0 is 零 líng.

  • 幺三八零零yāo sān bā líng líng — 1-3-8-0-0 (start of a mobile number)
  • 你的电话号码是多少？nǐ de diàn huà hào mǎ shì duō shao? — What is your phone number?

Ordinals and floors

Add 第 dì for 'the nth': 第一 dì yī (first), 第三 dì sān (third). Floors are the exception — 一楼 yī lóu is the ground floor, and buildings often skip floors containing 4 because 四 sì sounds like 死 sǐ (to die).

常见问题 · FAQ

Why is two sometimes 二 and sometimes 两?

两 goes before a measure word (两个人, 两杯咖啡). 二 is used for counting, dates, phone numbers, and in numbers like 二十. Before 十 it is always 二.

What is 万 in English?

Ten thousand. Chinese groups by four digits, so a million is 一百万 (one hundred ten-thousands), and there is no single word for million.

How do I read 19.9 yuan?

十九块九 — nineteen kuai nine. The unit after the decimal is 毛 for one digit, so 8.50 is 八块五.

Is 一 always pronounced yī?

No. Before a fourth tone it becomes yí (一个 yí ge); before first, second or third tone it becomes yì (一天 yì tiān). When counting it stays yī.
