How to Say Hello in Chinese

你好 is correct — and often the wrong choice. Here is what people actually say, by time of day and by relationship.

你好 nǐ hǎo is the first phrase every learner meets, and it is genuinely useful — with strangers, in shops, at the start of a language exchange. It is also slightly formal and slightly textbook, which is why a friend you message every day will not open with it. Chinese greetings lean on context: the time of day, whether you have met before, and whether you are speaking or typing.

The default: 你好 and 您好

您 is the polite 'you'. It matters more than learners expect: using 你 with a much older person or a customer is a small rudeness, and using 您 with a close friend is a small joke. When in doubt in a service or professional setting, use 您好.

  • 你好！nǐ hǎo! — Hello (neutral, works almost anywhere)
  • 您好！nín hǎo! — Hello (polite — to elders, clients, officials)
  • 大家好！dà jiā hǎo! — Hello everyone (to a group)

By time of day

ChinesePinyinWhen
早上好zǎo shang hǎoMorning, roughly before 10am
上午好shàng wǔ hǎoLate morning, 10am–noon
中午好zhōng wǔ hǎoAround midday
下午好xià wǔ hǎoAfternoon
晚上好wǎn shang hǎoEvening
晚安wǎn ānGoodnight — only when parting at night
晚安 is a farewell, not a greeting. Saying it when you arrive at 8pm gets a puzzled look.

Casual and in-between

  • 嗨 hāi / 嘿 hēi — borrowed from English, common among younger speakers and in messages.
  • 喂 wèi — only on the phone. Say it when you pick up, exactly like 'hello?'.
  • 好久不见 hǎo jiǔ bú jiàn — 'long time no see', for someone you have not met in months.
  • 最近怎么样？ zuì jìn zěn me yàng? — 'how have you been lately?', a real conversation starter.
  • 早 zǎo — a clipped 'morning', used between colleagues who pass each other daily.

Greetings that are not 'hello'

Chinese daily life uses observations instead of the word hello, and this is where learners feel the culture gap most.

  • 吃了吗？chī le ma? — Have you eaten? (friendly, not an invitation)
  • 去哪儿？qù nǎr? — Where are you off to? (casual, not nosy)
  • 上班啊？shàng bān a? — Off to work? (said to a neighbour)
  • 忙吗？máng ma? — Busy? (before asking for something)
These are phatic — greetings, not questions. 'Not yet' or 'just out for a walk' is a complete answer. Do not launch into a full account of your day.

Saying goodbye

  • 再见zài jiàn — Goodbye (standard)
  • 拜拜bái bái — Bye (casual, very common)
  • 明天见míng tiān jiàn — See you tomorrow
  • 回头见huí tóu jiàn — See you later
  • 慢走màn zǒu — Take care (said by host/shop staff as you leave)

Two things to get right

Tone sandhi. 你好 is written nǐ hǎo, both third tone, but you say ní hǎo — the first one rises. Nobody will misunderstand you either way; getting it right is what makes it sound natural.

你好吗？ nǐ hǎo ma? Most textbooks teach it. Most Chinese speakers do not say it. It reads like 'how do you do' in English: correct, dated, and slightly translated. Use 最近怎么样 or 你最近好吗 instead.

常见问题 · FAQ

Is 你好 rude or too formal?

Neither. It is neutral and safe with strangers, in shops, and in writing. Between close friends it can sound slightly distant, the way 'good day' does in English.

How do I greet an older person or a teacher?

Use 您好 and add the title: 王老师，您好. Dropping 您 with someone clearly senior to you reads as careless rather than friendly.

Why do people ask 吃了吗 instead of hello?

It is a phatic greeting, roughly 'how are you getting on'. It is not an invitation to dinner, and 'not yet' is a complete answer.

Should I use 你好吗?

Rarely. It is grammatically fine and textbook-common, but native speakers mostly say 最近怎么样 or 你最近好吗 instead.
