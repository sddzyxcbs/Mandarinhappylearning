How to Order Food in Chinese

From 'table for two' to 'doggy bag', the phrases that get you fed — with spice levels, dietary limits and the bill.

Restaurant Chinese is one of the best places to start speaking, because the script is predictable: someone asks how many, hands you a menu, takes your order, brings the bill. Learn ten exchanges and you can eat anywhere from a noodle shop to a banquet hall.

Getting seated

Answer with 位 wèi rather than 个 ge in a restaurant — it is the polite measure word for people and it is what the staff used.

  • 几位？jǐ wèi? — How many (people)? — what staff ask you first
  • 两位。liǎng wèi. — Two, please.
  • 有位置吗？yǒu wèi zhi ma? — Do you have a table?
  • 我们想坐靠窗的位置。wǒ men xiǎng zuò kào chuāng de wèi zhi. — We'd like a table by the window.

The menu

  • 菜单cài dān — menu
  • 有英文菜单吗？yǒu Yīngwén cài dān ma? — Do you have an English menu?
  • 有什么推荐的？yǒu shén me tuī jiàn de? — What do you recommend?
  • 招牌菜是什么？zhāo pai cài shì shén me? — What's your signature dish?
  • 这个是什么？zhè ge shì shén me? — What is this?

Ordering

来 lái ('bring') is the workhorse verb for ordering: 来一碗面 — a bowl of noodles, please. 要 yào is more direct. Adding 请 qǐng or 麻烦你 má fan nǐ softens either.

  • 我要一个…wǒ yào yí ge… — I'd like one…
  • 请给我…qǐng gěi wǒ… — Please give me…
  • 来两个啤酒。lái liǎng ge pí jiǔ. — Two beers, please.
  • 再来一份米饭。zài lái yí fèn mǐ fàn. — Another bowl of rice, please.
  • 够了，谢谢。gòu le, xiè xie. — That's enough, thanks.

In Sichuan and Hunan, 微辣 is still hot by most visitors' standards. If you mean 'not spicy at all', say 一点辣都不要 — not even a little.

Dietary limits

Vegetarianism is less common in mainland China than in many other places, so 素 dishes may still be cooked with meat stock or lard. 全素 (fully vegetarian) and 不要放肉 (no meat) are worth adding if it matters.

  • 我吃素。wǒ chī sù. — I'm vegetarian.
  • 我不吃肉。wǒ bù chī ròu. — I don't eat meat.
  • 我对花生过敏。wǒ duì huā shēng guò mǐn. — I'm allergic to peanuts.
  • 里面有猪肉吗？lǐ miàn yǒu zhū ròu ma? — Does it contain pork?

Drinks and extras

  • 一杯热水yì bēi rè shuǐ — a glass of hot water (the default in many restaurants)
  • 一瓶矿泉水yì píng kuàng quán shuǐ — a bottle of mineral water
  • 有纸巾吗？yǒu zhǐ jīn ma? — Do you have tissues? (often charged separately)
  • 再来点米饭zài lái diǎn mǐ fàn — a bit more rice

Paying

Mobile payment is dominant in mainland China; many small restaurants no longer keep cash. 发票 fāpiào is the official tax receipt, and it is a different document from a printed order slip.

  • 买单。mǎi dān. — The bill, please. (southern China)
  • 结账。jié zhàng. — The bill, please. (northern China)
  • 多少钱？duō shao qián? — How much?
  • 可以刷卡吗？kě yǐ shuā kǎ ma? — Can I pay by card?
  • 可以用微信支付吗？kě yǐ yòng Wēi xìn zhī fù ma? — Can I pay with WeChat Pay?
  • 我要发票。wǒ yào fā piào. — I need a receipt (official tax invoice).

Three phrases that carry the whole meal

  • 麻烦你 má fan nǐ — 'sorry to trouble you', the universal polite opener before any request.
  • 一下 yí xià — softens a verb: 看一下 (take a look), 等一下 (wait a moment). Requests with 一下 sound much less blunt.
  • 谢谢 xiè xie — said to staff on the way out, not after every plate.

常见问题 · FAQ

How do I say 'not spicy'?

不要辣 or 不辣. If you want reassurance, add 一点辣都不要 — not even a little. 微辣 still means mild spice, which is hot for many visitors.

Is it rude to call the waiter 服务员?

It is common and not offensive, but it can sound like summoning. 你好 or 麻烦你 plus eye contact is softer, and 老板 works in small shops.

How do I ask for the bill?

买单 (southern China) or 结账 (northern China). Add 谢谢, and expect to pay at the counter in smaller restaurants.

Should I tip?

No. Tipping is not customary in mainland China. Service charges appear only in upscale hotels and some foreign restaurants.
